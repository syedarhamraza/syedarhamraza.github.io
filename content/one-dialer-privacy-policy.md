# One Dialer — Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 30 October 2026_

One Dialer is a phone app for Android made by Syed Arham Raza. This policy explains what the app accesses and what it does with it.

## What One Dialer accesses, and why

When you make One Dialer your default phone app, Android gives it access to:

| Data | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| **Phone** (calls, SIM cards) | Making, answering, holding and ending calls; choosing which SIM to call from |
| **Call history** | Showing Recents, missed calls and blocked calls; deleting calls you choose to delete |
| **Contacts** | Showing names and photos for calls, contact search, favourites and speed dial; importing contacts from your SIM card or a .vcf file you pick, exporting them to a .vcf you share, and merging duplicates (Manage contacts) |
| **Notifications** | Showing incoming, ongoing and missed calls |
| **Nearby devices** (Bluetooth) | Showing the names of your connected Bluetooth devices (a watch, earbuds) so you can pick which one a call uses; nothing is sent anywhere |
| **Blocked numbers** | Blocking and unblocking numbers you choose |

## Where your data goes

**Your calls, call history and contacts never leave your phone.** One Dialer has no account and no servers, and it doesn't sell, share or upload any of this data. Settings you choose in the app (speed dial, blocking rules, quick decline messages, the SIM to use for a contact) are stored only on your phone.

"Decline with message" sends the text you pick as an SMS to the caller, through your carrier, only when you choose to.

## Crash and performance reports

If the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics (Google) so the bug can be fixed. A crash report contains technical details only: the app version, your phone model and Android version, and where in the app's code the crash happened. It does **not** include phone numbers, contact names, call history or messages. See [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

The app also sends limited performance diagnostics when it detects a severe slowdown or suspected freeze. You can send recent diagnostics yourself using Settings › Help & feedback › Report lag. These reports include screen and action names, performance timings, visual-effects settings, device and app versions, and a technical stack trace when available. Random report and session IDs help match a report to your feedback. They do not contain dialled digits, phone numbers, contact names or IDs, call history, or messages.

Pending diagnostics are stored privately on the phone, excluded from backups, for up to 24 hours (removed on the next maintenance pass while the app is running), with a limit of 20 reports and 256 KB. Firebase may retain a report locally after accepting it and upload it later when a connection is available. A queued confirmation does not mean the server has received the report. Automatic reporting is rate-limited to avoid excessive collection.

## Children

One Dialer is not directed at children under 13.
