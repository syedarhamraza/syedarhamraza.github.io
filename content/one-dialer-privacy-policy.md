# One Dialer — Privacy Policy

_Last updated: 10 November 2026_

One Dialer is a phone app for Android made by Syed Arham Raza. This policy explains what the app accesses and what it does with it.

## What One Dialer accesses, and why

When you make One Dialer your default phone app, Android gives it access to:

| Data | Used for |
| --- | --- |
| **Phone** (calls, SIM cards) | Making, answering, holding and ending calls; choosing which SIM to call from |
| **Call history** | Showing Recents, missed calls and blocked calls; deleting calls you choose to delete |
| **Contacts** | Showing names and photos for calls, contact search, favourites and speed dial; importing contacts from your SIM card or a .vcf file you pick, exporting them to a .vcf you share, and merging duplicates (Manage contacts) |
| **Notifications** | Showing incoming, ongoing and missed calls |
| **Nearby devices** (Bluetooth) | Showing the names of your connected Bluetooth devices (a watch, earbuds) so you can pick which one a call uses; nothing is sent anywhere |
| **Blocked numbers** | Blocking and unblocking numbers you choose |

## Where your data goes

**Your calls, call history and contacts never leave your phone.** One Dialer has no account and no servers, and it doesn't sell, share or upload any of this data. Settings you choose in the app (speed dial, blocking rules, quick decline messages, the SIM to use for a contact) are stored only on your phone.

**Backups.** If you have turned on your phone's own backup (Google's, or your phone maker's), Android includes One Dialer's settings, call notes and call backgrounds in it, so they come back when you reinstall the app or move to a new phone. That backup belongs to your Google or phone-maker account; One Dialer never sees or controls it. Settings › Export settings saves the same data to a file wherever you choose, and Import settings reads it back. One Dialer never uploads that file.

When Google Play has an update for One Dialer, Software update downloads that update's release notes from this website (syedarhamraza.github.io). Nothing about you or your phone is sent with the request.

**Call recording** needs the separate One Dialer Recorder app, which is downloaded from this website and is not part of the Google Play app. One Dialer itself never uses the microphone. When you record a call, the audio is saved only inside One Dialer Recorder on your phone. It is never uploaded, and it is deleted when you delete it or uninstall the recorder. One Dialer Recorder asks you to turn on an accessibility service: Android only lets apps with one use the microphone during a call. The service reads nothing on your screen. Recording laws differ by country, and you are responsible for getting the other person's consent where it's required.

"Decline with SMS" sends the text you pick as an SMS to the caller, through your carrier, only when you choose to.

## Crash, usage and performance reports

If the app crashes, it sends a crash report to Firebase Crashlytics (Google) so the bug can be fixed. A crash report contains technical details only: the app version, your phone model and Android version, and where in the app's code the crash happened. It does **not** include phone numbers, contact names, call history or messages. See [Google's privacy policy](https://policies.google.com/privacy).

The app also sends usage statistics to Firebase Analytics (Google): when the app is installed, opened and updated, how long it is used, and which of its screens (main app or call screen) is open. This data includes the app version, phone model, Android version, language, the country (worked out from the IP address) and a Firebase app instance ID. It does **not** include phone numbers, contact names, call history or messages, and it does not use your advertising ID. Google keeps it for 2 months.

Beta versions also send performance data to Firebase Performance Monitoring (Google): how long the app takes to start, how smoothly its screens draw, and how long the release-notes request takes. This data includes the app version, phone model, Android version, network type, the country (worked out from the IP address) and a Firebase installation ID. It does **not** include phone numbers, contact names, call history or messages. Performance Monitoring will be removed before the app leaves beta.

The app also sends limited performance diagnostics when it detects a severe slowdown or suspected freeze. You can send recent diagnostics yourself using Settings › Help & feedback › Report lag. These reports include screen and action names, performance timings, visual-effects settings, how many taps reached the app (counts only, never where you tapped), the device's chip, device and app versions, and a technical stack trace when available. Random report and session IDs help match a report to your feedback. They do not contain dialled digits, phone numbers, contact names or IDs, call history, or messages.

Pending diagnostics are stored privately on the phone, excluded from backups, for up to 24 hours (removed on the next maintenance pass while the app is running), with a limit of 20 reports and 256 KB. Firebase may retain a report locally after accepting it and upload it later when a connection is available. A queued confirmation does not mean the server has received the report. Automatic reporting is rate-limited to avoid excessive collection.
